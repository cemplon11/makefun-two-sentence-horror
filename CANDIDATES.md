# Two-Sentence Horror — candidates

## A (PICKED)
Since we moved in, my wife has sleepwalked to the kitchen every night, and each morning I find one more chair pulled out from the table. This morning there were six chairs pulled out, and we only own five.

Why: quiet, concrete, countable dread. Sentence 1 sets a nightly ritual with a small wrong detail that grows; sentence 2 turns it — the count exceeds what exists in the house, so someone (or something) else has been sitting down to dinner with them, night after night, and the sleepwalking wife may be the one setting its place. Nothing is explained, no gore, no familiar twist.

## B
My dentist blamed the toothache on grinding and fitted me with a plastic night guard. This morning the guard was bitten clean through, and the teeth marks in it were smaller than mine.

Why not picked: original image and concrete, but the scare lands in the body rather than in the house, and the second sentence explains its own turn a little too neatly.

## C
I keep the baby monitor on all night so I can hear my daughter breathing in the next room. Last night I heard her breathing on the monitor and, at the same moment, felt her small hand on my shoulder behind me.

Why not picked: strong turn (if she is behind me, whose breathing is in her room?), but the baby-monitor setup is a more familiar horror trope than the counting chairs.
